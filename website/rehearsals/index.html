Rehearsals

Learn a tune as a band. Each player records their own part on their own phone or computer, over the same click, and every device ends up with all the parts.

  • Start a tune
  • Record your part
  • Measure the delay
  • Bring the band in
  • Play along to a recording
  • Slow it down
  • Chords and scores

Start a tune

One person creates the tune. The others join it later.

  1. Open the Rehearse tab and tap Start a tune.
  2. Fill in Tune, Your name and Your instrument.
  3. Set the Tempo, the Time signature (2/4 to 7/4) and the Count-in: none, 1, 2 or 4 bars. Two bars is the default.
  4. Tap Create.

The tune opens, with a lane for your part.

Record your part

Play over the click and the parts already there.

  1. To add another lane, tap + (Add a part) and pick an instrument.
  2. Put on headphones.
  3. Tap the red record button on your lane. The count-in plays, then recording starts.
  4. Tap the same button (now a square) to stop.

Your take joins the mix. Not happy? Record over it, or open More on the lane and tap Delete this recording.

You can only record and delete your own parts. A deleted take also disappears from the other devices the next time they sync.

Without headphones

On the phone's speaker, the microphone hears the click and the other parts, and they end up in your take. On Android, a take recorded this way can be cleaned afterwards: open More and tap Remove the speaker bleed. The original is kept.

Record the rack instead of the microphone

To record an instrument from the rack (a soundfont, a VST3 plugin, the drum generator), open More on your lane and tick Record from the rack. The sound goes straight into the take, so there is no delay to correct. Not in the web demo.

Measure the delay

Every device takes a moment to play a sound and another to record it. Without a correction, your takes land slightly after the beat. The app measures this delay once and removes it from every take.

  1. When a tune shows a banner saying the delay has not been measured, tap Calibrate. You can also go to Preferences, under About, and tap Overdub latency.
  2. Use the speaker, not headphones: the microphone must hear the test sounds. Turn the volume up and keep the room quiet.
  3. Tap Measure now. It takes a few seconds.

The figure belongs to what you listen on, not to the tune. The speaker, wired headphones and each Bluetooth headset have their own, and one measurement counts for every tune. Bluetooth adds a long delay: to measure a Bluetooth headset, hold one earcup against the phone's microphone while the test runs.

Bring the band in

The others get the tune on their own device, directly from yours.

The steps are in the guide: Join a rehearsal. In short, you tap the share icon in the tune, and the others scan the code (on a phone) or paste the link (on a computer).

You only do this once. After that, the devices find each other on their own when you all open the same tune.

  • Each lane shows whether its player is Here now or Not connected.
  • A take marked Not sent yet has not reached everyone. Stay connected until the mark goes away.
  • Someone who missed a rehearsal gets the new parts the next time you are all on the same network.

When the room has no Wi-Fi

Everyone must be on the same network, and phones on mobile data cannot see each other. One person turns on their phone's hotspot and the others join it. The parts go from phone to phone, encrypted, with no server and no account, so the rehearsal uses no mobile data.

Play along to a recording

Import the song you are learning, and record your parts over it.

  1. In the tune, tap Play along to a recording and pick a file. MP3, FLAC and WAV work everywhere. On Android, M4A, AAC, OGG, Opus and video files work too (only their sound is used).
  2. Align the grid opens. Play the recording and tap Tap the beat in time with it.
  3. Drag the marker onto the first downbeat of the song.
  4. Zoom in on the waveform. The red lines are the beats: if they drift away from the sound, use Slower by 0.1 or Faster by 0.1 until they stay on it.
  5. Tap Check two bars to listen, then Done.

The bars line up with the song. The click turns off, because the recording now sets the tempo.

Align the grid before anyone records: once a part is recorded, the tempo is locked.

Slow it down

Tap the tempo at the top of the tune (it shows the BPM and the time signature).

  • Practice speed plays everything slower, down to 50%, without changing the pitch. It only affects your device: the rest of the band keeps their own speed.
  • Tune tempo changes the tempo for everyone, from 40 to 240 BPM. The recordings are adjusted to match. When the tune has an imported recording, the tempo comes from it and you change it in Align the grid.
  • The Count-in can be changed here too.

The time signature cannot change once a part is recorded.

Chords and scores

Chord chart

Tap Write the chords above the lanes, then tap a bar to type its chords, for example Am7, C#m7, Gm7b5 or C6/9. Chords per bar offers the splits that land on a beat: in 4/4, one, two or four chords. While the tune plays, the current bar lights up, and the one-line view scrolls along with it.

Scores

Tap the book icon at the top of the tune (Scores), then Add a score: a PDF, a chart, a photo of a page. Everyone in the tune gets a copy. Photos open full screen and you can zoom in. Other files open in the app your device normally uses for them.
