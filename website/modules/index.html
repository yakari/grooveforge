Every module you can add with the + button: what it does, where its cables go, and its main controls.

New to cables? Read How sound moves between modules first. In short: an instrument plays through the speakers on its own, yellow cables carry notes, and red and white cables carry sound into an effect.

Modules marked Apps only work in the Android, Linux, macOS and Windows apps. In the web demo they are greyed out.

GrooveForge Keyboard

A keyboard that plays a soundfont (an .sf2 file of instrument sounds). A new project starts with one. Pick its sound from the soundfont and patch menus on the module; add more soundfonts in Preferences. It plays the notes of its MIDI channel, shown on the MIDI CH badge, so a controller on that channel plays it too.

You hear it without any cable. Its MIDI OUT sends what you play to another instrument (Play the guitar from the keyboard), and its AUDIO OUT L/R go to an effect or a looper. Choose None (MIDI only) in the soundfont menu and the keyboard goes silent: it only sends notes. SCALE IN and TUNE IN are where Xen or Jam Mode plug in to lock it to a scale. CHORD OUT tells Jam Mode, Xen or a harmonizer which chord you are holding.

Vocoder

Makes a synth talk. Hold notes on its keyboard and speak or sing into the microphone: the synth takes the shape of your voice. It listens to the input chosen in Preferences. Wear headphones, or the microphone hears the speaker. Apps only.

Pick the synth sound with Saw, Square or Choral. Harmony drops the synth and moves your own voice to the notes you hold. Like the keyboard, it has MIDI IN, MIDI OUT, SCALE IN and TUNE IN, and plays through the speakers without a cable.

Riff

An electric guitar, modelled string by string, so it bends, mutes and rings like one. Apps only.

Riff has no keys. Cable a keyboard's MIDI OUT into Riff's MIDI IN (step by step), then Riff's AUDIO OUT L/R into the Amp for crunch (step by step). Each note goes to a string a guitarist would use, and close notes take separate strings so they ring together.

  • Palm mute, Pick pos. and Attack are the picking hand. They change strings that are already ringing, so map them to a pedal or a knob (Map a knob).
  • Legato slurs notes played close together: hammer-ons going up, pull-offs going down, on one string. A pad sending CC 68 does the same while you hold it. Overlap slurs only keys you press before releasing the last one.
  • The pitch wheel bends by the Bend range (2 semitones by default, up to 12). The mod wheel adds vibrato.
  • Pickup (Neck, Middle, Bridge, Neck+Bridge), Type (Single coil, Humbucker) and Tone shape the sound.
  • The sustain pedal lets released strings ring; Pedal sus. sets for how long. Feedback makes held strings swell into the Harmonic you pick, like a guitar in front of a loud amp.

Stylophone

A 25-key metal strip: touch or slide along it to play, one note at a time. Sliding from key to key glides without a click. SQR, SAW, SIN and TRI pick the waveform, VIB adds vibrato, and the octave buttons move the strip up or down. ARP, DUTY, NOISE, CRUSH and SUB give it an 8-bit game sound.

Its MIDI OUT can play another instrument. Press MUTE to silence the Stylophone itself while it keeps sending notes.

Theremin

Play without keys. In PAD mode, slide up and down for pitch and left and right for volume. In CAM mode (Android, iOS and macOS), the front camera measures how far your hand is: closer plays higher. The camera image is not saved or sent anywhere; see the privacy policy.

BASE sets the lowest note, RANGE the span in octaves, VIB the vibrato and HEIGHT the size of the pad. Like the Stylophone, it has a MIDI OUT and a MUTE button.

Jam Mode

Listens to the chords you play on one keyboard and keeps other keyboards in the matching scale, so a second player cannot hit a wrong note. One per project. To choose the scale yourself instead, use Xen.

  1. Under MASTER, pick the keyboard you play chords on.
  2. Under TARGETS, tap Add and pick the keyboards (or vocoders) to lock.
  3. Switch the module on with its round ON button and pick a SCALE TYPE, such as Blues or Dorian.

The same links show in Patch view as purple cables: the master's CHORD OUT into Jam Mode's CHORD IN, and Jam Mode's SCALE OUT into each target's SCALE IN. Drawing a cable or picking in the menu does the same thing. DETECT reads the whole Chord or only the lowest note (Bass). SYNC waits for the next beat, half bar or bar before changing scale.

Drum Generator

A drummer that follows the tempo at the top of the screen.

Pick a Style, from bossa nova to metal, and switch Active on. If the tempo was stopped, it starts. Swing pushes the off-beats late, and Human feel goes from Robotic to Live drummer. Count-in gives you one or two bars, or four stick hits, before the beat starts, and Fill every adds a fill every 4, 8 or 16 bars.

It plays through the speakers on its own. To record it in the Audio Looper or add an effect, cable its AUDIO OUT L/R. Load .gfdrum… opens your own patterns: see Rhythmic patterns.

MIDI Looper

Records the notes you play, not the sound, and plays them back in a loop. One per project.

  1. In Patch view, cable the keyboard's MIDI OUT into the looper's MIDI IN: that is what it records.
  2. Cable the looper's MIDI OUT into the instrument that should play the loop, often the same keyboard's MIDI IN.
  3. Press Record. Recording waits for the next bar, and the loop length is rounded to whole bars.

Each layer you add with Overdub is a track with its own Mute, Reverse and speed buttons (½×, 1×, 2×). Quantize snaps the recorded notes to the beat. Because the looper stores notes, you can change the sound after recording.

Audio Looper

Records the sound of an instrument, a voice or a whole effects chain, and loops it. Apps only.

It records only what reaches its AUDIO IN L/R. Cable an instrument into it, or pick one in its Source menu, which draws the cables for you. The steps are in Record a loop.

With Bar sync on, recording starts on the next downbeat and stops at the end of a bar, so loops stay in time. Overdub records a new layer on top, Reverse plays it backwards and Clear empties it. The loop plays straight to the speakers: the looper has no audio output to cable.

Live Input

Brings a microphone, a guitar or any line input into the rack. Cable its AUDIO OUT L/R into an effect, such as the Autotune, the Amp or the Audio Harmonizer, or into the Audio Looper. Apps only.

The input itself is chosen in Preferences › Audio Input. On the module, Gain sets the level, and 1+2, L or R picks which channel of the input you use. Mute local monitor (still feeds effects) stops you hearing the dry signal. On Android, Live Input mutes itself when the phone's own microphone would feed back through the phone's speaker. Tap Unmute anyway to override.

Effects

Every effect has AUDIO IN L/R and AUDIO OUT L/R: cable an instrument's outputs into its inputs, as in Add an effect. What comes out of the effect goes to the speakers. The power button at the top of an effect switches it off (Bypass) so you can compare. All built-in effects are Apps only. Knob names appear in English in every language.

Plate Reverb

Puts the sound in a room. Room sets its size, Damp darkens the tail, Width spreads it left and right, and Mix sets how much reverb you hear.

Ping-Pong Delay

Repeats what you play, with echoes bouncing between left and right. Time sets the gap (1 to 2000 ms). Switch BPM on and pick a Div, from 2 bars to 1/16, to lock the echoes to the tempo. Feedback sets how many repeats, Mix how loud they are.

Wah

The "wah-wah" guitar sound. In Auto mode it sweeps by itself: Rate and Wave set the speed and shape, or BPM and Div lock it to the tempo. In Manual mode, Pedal moves it: map Pedal to an expression pedal (Map a knob) and you have a wah pedal. Center, Reso and Depth set where it sweeps and how sharp it sounds.

4-Band EQ

Turns bass, mids and treble up or down, by up to 12 dB per band. Low and High act on everything below or above their frequency; the two middle bands (LM, HM) act around theirs, and their Q knob sets how narrow. Flat resets every band.

Compressor

Evens out loud and quiet notes. Sound above Thresh is turned down by the Ratio (4:1 by default). Attack and Release set how fast it reacts, and Makeup brings the level back up afterwards.

Chorus / Flanger

Thickens the sound by mixing in a slightly delayed copy that wobbles. Rate and Depth set the wobble (or BPM and Div lock it to the tempo). For a flanger's jet sound, lower Delay and raise FB.

Audio Harmonizer

Adds up to four pitch-shifted copies of any sound: sing one note, hear a chord.

Cable an instrument or a Live Input into it. Voices sets how many copies play. Each voice (V1 to V4) has an interval, up to two octaves up or down, and a Level. Dry/Wet balances your own sound against the harmony.

Scale Lock keeps the harmony inside a scale. It needs to know which one: cable the SCALE OUT of Xen or Jam Mode into the harmonizer's SCALE IN. To set the intervals by playing, cable a keyboard's CHORD OUT into its CHORD IN and hold a chord: the lowest note stands for your voice, and the notes above it become the voices.

Autotune

Pulls your voice onto the notes of a scale, from a light touch to the robot effect. Cable a Live Input into it and sing. The display shows the note you sing, how many cents off it is, and the note it is pulled to.

Set the Key and Scale, or cable Xen's or Jam Mode's SCALE OUT into its SCALE IN to follow that scale instead. Retune sets how fast the note is corrected: 0 ms jumps straight to it, which is the robot sound; slower settings sound natural. Strength sets how far it pulls, Humanize lets long notes keep their vibrato, Flex leaves notes far from the scale (slides, blue notes) alone, and the transpose bar shifts your voice up to an octave up or down. Mix blends in your untouched voice.

Amp

A drive pedal, a guitar amp and a speaker cabinet, in the order a guitarist plugs them in.

Cable Riff into it (step by step), or a real guitar through Live Input. Each section has its own On switch, which you can map to a footswitch.

  • Drive: a pedal, off by default. Type is Overdrive (pushes the mids, keeps the bass tight) or Distortion (rougher), with its own Drive and Tone knobs.
  • Amp: Model (US, Brit or AC), Gain from clean to crunch, then Bass, Middle and Treble, which affect each other as on the real amps.
  • Cab: the Cabinet, 1x12, 2x12 or 4x12, or an IR file (below).
  • Level sets the output volume.

Cabinet impulse responses

Much of a guitar sound comes from the speaker cabinet. An impulse response (IR) is a small .wav recording of a real cabinet and its microphone. Tap Load IR under the Amp and pick a file: the Cabinet selector switches to IR. The IR is saved inside your project, so it travels with it.

Most uncompressed WAV files work, at any sample rate. For a stereo file, only the first channel is used, and only the first 100 ms, which is where a cabinet's sound lives. Free IRs to start with:

  • TONE3000: a community library with thousands of free IRs
  • Kalthallen Cabs: free and paid packs of classic cabinets
  • Celestion: a free IR from the speaker maker

Each pack has its own licence: free to play through, but usually not to share, which is why GrooveForge cannot ship them. Download them yourself from these sites.

VST3 plugins

Desktop only (Linux, macOS and Windows). Load a synth or an effect made by someone else. In the + sheet, Pick Installed Instrument and Pick Installed Effect list the plugins found on your computer; Browse VST3 Instrument… and Browse VST3 Effect… open a .vst3 folder anywhere else. A VST3 instrument is cabled like the keyboard, a VST3 effect like a built-in effect. More on the VST3 page.

Your own plugins

Load .gfpd from file…, at the bottom of the + sheet, adds an effect or a MIDI FX described in a .gfpd text file. See Plugin descriptors.
