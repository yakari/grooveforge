Getting started

Short recipes, each with a clip of the real app. Watch it once, then do the same on your device.

How sound moves between modules

Your rack is a stack of modules: instruments, effects, loopers, a drum machine. Tap the cable icon at the top of the screen (Patch view) and you see the back of each module, with sockets you connect with cables, like on real gear.

Three rules cover almost everything:

  1. An instrument plays through the speakers on its own. You do not need a cable to hear it.
  2. Yellow cables carry notes (MIDI) from a keyboard to an instrument. Red and white cables carry sound, left and right.
  3. To put an effect after an instrument, cable the instrument's AUDIO OUT L and R into the effect's AUDIO IN L and R. What comes out of the effect goes to the speakers.

To draw a cable, long-press a socket and drag to another one. Sockets that would accept it light up while you drag.

Play in a scale

Every key you press lands in the scale you chose, so nothing sounds wrong.

  1. A new project has a keyboard and a Xen module. Xen starts switched off.
  2. Tap ON in the Xen panel.
  3. Hold a key on the keyboard and tap a scale, for example Blues. The key you hold becomes the root note.
  4. To try other traditions, open the Family menu: Maqam, Raga, Japanese scales and more.

Grey keys are outside the scale. Press one and you hear the nearest note that fits. Some scales use notes between the piano keys: those keys get a teal band.

Add an effect to your keyboard

Here the keyboard goes through an Audio Harmonizer. Reverb, Delay, Wah or EQ work the same way.

  1. Tap + and pick the effect.
  2. Tap the cable icon at the top to open Patch view.
  3. Long-press the keyboard's red AUDIO OUT L and drag it to the effect's AUDIO IN L.
  4. Do the same with the white AUDIO OUT R and AUDIO IN R.
  5. Tap the back arrow and play.

You hear the keyboard through the effect. Turn the effect's knobs to change the sound.

Two instruments cabled straight into the same effect are mixed and go through it together. If GrooveForge refuses a cable to an effect, that effect already gets a different signal: add a second copy of it.

Remove a cable or a module

Undo a connection, or take a module out of the rack.

A cable

  1. In Patch view, tap the small ✕ in the middle of the cable.
  2. Tap Disconnect.

A module

  1. Tap the bin icon at the top of the module.
  2. Tap Remove. Its cables go with it.

Play the guitar from the keyboard

Riff is a modelled electric guitar. It has no keys of its own: a keyboard sends it the notes.

  1. Tap + and pick Riff.
  2. Open Patch view. Long-press the keyboard's yellow MIDI OUT and drag it to Riff's MIDI IN.
  3. Go back and play: you hear the piano and the guitar together.
  4. To hear only the guitar, open the keyboard's sound menu and choose None (MIDI only). The keyboard goes silent and only sends notes.

The on-screen keyboard plays Riff. The same works for any instrument with a MIDI IN.

Put the guitar through the amp

The Amp module adds a drive pedal, an amp and a speaker cabinet.

  1. Tap + and pick Amp.
  2. In Patch view, drag Riff's AUDIO OUT L to the Amp's AUDIO IN L, then R to R.
  3. Go back, play, and turn Drive on the Amp.

The clean guitar becomes a crunchy or distorted one, depending on the Drive and the amp you pick.

Connect a MIDI controller

Play with a real keyboard or keytar instead of the screen.

  1. Plug the controller in with USB (an OTG adapter on a phone), or switch on a Bluetooth MIDI controller.
  2. Tap the gear icon to open Preferences.
  3. Under MIDI Connection, tap Connect MIDI Device and pick your controller.

Your controller plays the module whose MIDI CH badge matches the channel it sends on, usually channel 1. Tap the badge on a module to change its channel.

Map a knob to a control

Turn a knob or press a pad on your controller to move a control in the app.

  1. Tap the sliders icon at the top of the module.
  2. In the list, tap Learn next to the control you want, for example Palm mute.
  3. Move a knob or press a button on your controller.

The control now follows your hardware. The mapping is saved with the project.

Start a drum beat

A drummer that follows the tempo at the top of the screen.

  1. Tap + and pick Drum Generator.
  2. Pick a pattern from the menu, from bossa nova to metal.
  3. Switch Active on and press play at the top of the screen.

The beat plays at the tempo shown in the transport bar. Swing and Human feel change how it grooves.

Record a loop

Record a few bars, let them repeat, and play something new on top.

  1. Tap + and pick Audio Looper.
  2. In Patch view, cable the keyboard's AUDIO OUT L and R into the looper's AUDIO IN L and R.
  3. Go back and press the looper's record button. Recording begins on the first beat of the next bar. If the tempo was stopped, it starts and gives you one bar to get ready.
  4. Play. Press the button again: recording finishes at the end of the bar and the loop starts playing.
  5. Press it once more to record a second layer on top.

Your loop repeats in time, and you can play over it.

Save and open projects

A project keeps your modules, cables, settings, mappings and loops in one .gf file.

  1. Tap the folder icon at the top.
  2. Save As… writes the project to a file you name.
  3. New Project starts again from a keyboard and Xen. Anything you have not saved is lost.
  4. Open Project loads a .gf file, with every module and cable as you left them.

On a phone or a computer, the app also keeps your current session between launches. The web demo does not: use Save As… to download your project.

Join a rehearsal

Several people record their own part of the same tune, each on their own device.

  1. Put every device on the same Wi-Fi, or on one phone's hotspot.
  2. On the device that has the tune, open it and tap the share icon. It shows a code.
  3. On yours, open the Rehearse tab, tap Join a tune, then Scan a code. No camera? Paste the link the other device gives you.
  4. Enter your name and your instrument, then tap Join.

The tune and its parts appear on your device. You record your own part and nobody else's.

Playing along to an imported track? Align the grid lines the bars up with it: tap along to the beat, then drag the marker onto the first downbeat. More on the Rehearsals page.
